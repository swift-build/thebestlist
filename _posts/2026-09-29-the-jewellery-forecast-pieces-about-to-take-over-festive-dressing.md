Every festive season has its quiet protagonist. Some years it's the saree, some years the silhouette. This year, it's the jewellery. As Navratri gives way to Diwali and the long shimmer of wedding season, the pieces being unboxed, restrung and reimagined are doing the heavy lifting, and the outfits are stepping back to let them.

Consider this your forecast: what's rising, what's returning, and what's worth investing in before the invitations start arriving.

**1. The Sculptural Choker**

Forget the delicate, forgettable choker of seasons past. The shape of the moment is architectural: a broad, close-fitting collar in antique gold or oxidised silver, worked with beaten metal, kundan, or a single dramatic stone. It sits high on the collarbone and asks for a clean neckline, think a plain silk blouse or a structured high-neck. One choker, no layering, and the entire look is finished.

**2. The Jhumka, Enlarged**

The jhumka has never really left, but it's returning with scale and intent. Look for oversized domes in matte gold, tiered versions with pearl drops, and unexpected proportions that graze the jawline rather than the shoulder. The styling rule is simple: when the earrings are the statement, keep the neckline bare and the hair swept away.

**3. Polki, Pared Back**

Uncut diamonds remain the festive gold standard, but the way they're worn is shifting. Instead of the full parure, the stylish are choosing a single polki piece, a slim rivière, a pair of studs, a ring with real presence, and pairing it with textured silks and handloom drapes. It reads as confident rather than costume, and it travels from puja to dinner without a change.

**4. The Cuff and the Kada Revival**

Bangles have company. Wide cuffs and sculptural kadas, in gold, silver, and enamelled finishes, are moving to the forearm and upper arm, often worn solo. On the wrist, a single heavy kada beside a slim watch is the quiet-luxury version of the festive stack.

**5. Maang Tikka, Off-Centre**

Bridal by tradition, editorial by temperament. This season the maang tikka is being worn slightly off-centre or as a matha patti with a softer, unfussy drape, and not just by the bride. Wedding guests and festival hosts are borrowing it as the single dramatic accent against an otherwise minimal look.

**6. Pearls, Reclaimed**

Pearls are having a genuinely fresh moment in festive dressing: baroque shapes, mixed sizes, long ropes knotted casually over a kurta or drape. Paired with gold, they soften the formality of traditional pieces and lend an easy, inherited quality to the whole outfit.

**7. Coloured Stones, Confident Palettes**

Emeralds, rubies, and sapphires (or their beautifully executed lookalikes) are edging out the all-gold default. Deep green against rust silk, ruby against ivory, a lone sapphire drop against black: colour in jewellery is being used the way it is in fashion, as a deliberate, considered contrast.
