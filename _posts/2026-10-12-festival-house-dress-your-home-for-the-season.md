There is a particular kind of anticipation that settles over a house in the weeks before a festival. Cupboards are opened and aired. Brass is brought out for polishing. Somewhere in a back room, a box of diyas is found, counted and found slightly insufficient. Indian homes have always understood something that interior designers spend careers trying to articulate: that a space is never more beautiful than when it is being prepared for people.

This season, the calendar is generous. Navratri runs through 19 October, with Dussehra on 20 October, followed by Dhanteras on 6 November and Diwali on Sunday 8 November. That is the better part of a month of celebration, which is exactly why the smartest approach to dressing your home is to build in layers, not to redo it all at once.

### **A Month-Long Plan**

* **Now through Dussehra:**

   Flowers, torans and light. Keep it fresh and simple.
* **After Dussehra:**

   Deep clean, edit, polish the brass and plan the Diwali palette.
* **Dhanteras week:**

   Introduce the heavier layers: new textiles, centrepieces, a refreshed puja corner.
* **Diwali:**

   Full lighting, rangoli at the door, a laid table, and a home ready for guests.

### **The Finish**

The most beautiful festive homes are not the most decorated ones. They are the ones where every element has a reason: the diya lit for a prayer, the flowers that smell of a childhood house, the brass that belonged to someone’s grandmother. Dress your home for the season by dressing it for the people who will walk through the door, and the rest will take care of itself.
