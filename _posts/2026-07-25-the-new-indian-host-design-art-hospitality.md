There is a particular kind of Indian home that has quietly come into its own over the last decade. It is not the home of a single design era, nor is it built to impress from a distance. It is built to be lived in, gathered in, and remembered. Vaghani Residence, a South Mumbai residence conceived by designer Minnie Bhatt, belongs firmly to this new generation of homes where architecture, art and the instinct to host are treated as one continuous idea.

Walk through its grand entrance, past marble-clad walls and a sleek PU-finished main door, and the intent is immediate. This is a home built for arrival, for the moment guests step in and understand exactly what kind of evening awaits them. It is a small gesture with outsized consequence, the sort of detail that separates a house from a home that knows how to hold a room.



![](/assets/images/posts/vaughnresidence.jpg)



**Grandeur, Recalibrated for Real Life**

Neo-classical architecture has always carried a certain formality, columns and corniced ceilings speaking a language of permanence and occasion. What makes Vaghani Residence compelling is how that formality has been recalibrated for the way people actually live now. The living area, generous in height, is divided into formal and informal zones, a quiet acknowledgment that today's host needs both. There is space for the dinner party and space for the Sunday afternoon that stretches into evening.

The curved ceiling, with its intricate cornice detailing, adds a sense of movement to a room that could easily have felt static. Glass doors with metal arch detailing draw the eye toward the dining space beyond, creating a visual thread between rooms that invites guests to move rather than sit still. It is architecture designed with circulation and conversation in mind, which is, in essence, the architecture of good hosting.



![](/assets/images/posts/titlebannerartile12.jpg)



**The Table as Centrepiece**

If there is one element that captures the spirit of the home, it is the dining space. A bespoke black and white marble table, inlaid with precision, anchors the room beneath a crystal chandelier, while a Rothko-inspired console stands nearby as a study in restraint. This is a table built to hold court, surrounded by the kind of art that signals a homeowner who understands that hosting is as much about atmosphere as it is about menu.

This pairing of classical materiality with contemporary art is the throughline of the entire residence. Marble surfaces and decorative moldings sit comfortably alongside abstract art and modern furniture, proof that timeless interiors are rarely the product of a single aesthetic. They come from layering, from choosing pieces that can evolve with a household over years rather than seasons.



![](/assets/images/posts/vaughresidence223.jpg)



**Designing for the Way We Actually Gather**

What Vaghani Residence ultimately offers is a blueprint for a certain kind of Indian domestic life, one where formal entertaining and intimate family moments are given equal architectural weight. It is a reminder that the best homes are not designed around a single occasion but around the full range of what a household experiences, the large celebration and the quiet weeknight dinner alike.

As more Indian homeowners invest in spaces that need to perform both roles, Vaghani Residence stands as a study in how to do it without compromise. Classical grandeur meets contemporary ease, and the result is a home built not just to be admired, but to be lived in, fully and often.
