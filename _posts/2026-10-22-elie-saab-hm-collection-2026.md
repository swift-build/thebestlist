Few designers understand the language of celebration quite like Elie Saab. His gowns have long been the quiet protagonists of red carpets and wedding days alike: lace that seems spun from light, tulle that moves like water, sequins that catch every flash. On 22 October, that language reached a new audience as Elie Saab x H&M launched, bringing the house’s couture-inspired aesthetic to a more accessible high-street collection. 







**The Designer, the Retailer, the Moment**

It is the latest chapter in H&M’s long tradition of designer collaborations, and a fitting one for the moment. After seasons of quiet luxury and neutral basics, fashion has rediscovered the pleasure of dressing up, and Saab’s maximal, romantic vocabulary arrives just as the festive and wedding season begins.

The story behind the name matters, too. Saab founded his house in Beirut in 1982, made his name through bridal and eveningwear, and in 2003 became one of the first non-French designers admitted to the Chambre Syndicale de la Haute Couture. H&M’s own creative advisor, Ann-Sofie Johansson, framed the collaboration around that arc, describing the journey from master of bridalwear in Beirut to the centre of Paris fashion as an amazing, rich story.

**What’s in the Collection**

The collection spans womenswear, menswear, accessories and footwear, with embellished gowns, shimmering separates and tailored pieces, using fine lace, gradient sequins and signature monogram detailing. H&M’s promise to customers was a collection that is magical, ornate and glamorous, and the house codes are unmistakable: surface richness, fluid evening silhouettes and a flattering, sharply defined cut. 

*Saab himself was clear about the spirit of the project.* 

> “This collection is created for all who admire the world of Elie Saab,” 

he said, expressing the hope that it would be received with the same enthusiasm with which it was made. It is also reported to mark the designer’s first step into menswear. 



**A Paris Celebration**

Ahead of the launch, Saab marked the collaboration with an event at Paris’s historic Palais Brongniart, where guests included models Iris Law, Lila Moss and Amelia Gray Hamlin, and Spanish actor Manu Rios.

**Why It Matters for India**

India has long been one of Saab’s most devoted markets for occasionwear, particularly for weddings and celebrations. The house recently opened its first Indian boutique at DLF Emporio, New Delhi. For the Indian shopper, the collaboration is an invitation into that world without couture’s price tag: a statement piece for a sangeet, a cocktail evening or a festive dinner. 



**How to Wear It**

\- For the sangeet or cocktail: a sequinned or gradient-embellished gown needs little more than a clean updo and statement earrings.

\- For festive dinners: a shimmering separate worn with simple tailoring brings sparkle without the full gown.

\- For the wedding guest: lace or monogram detailing in a deep, jewel-toned palette photographs beautifully and layers well with traditional jewellery.

\- For him: a sharp, tailored piece lets the house’s polish do the talking.



**The Last Word**

At its best, a designer collaboration does more than democratise a label: it translates a point of view. Elie Saab x H&M asks a simple question, which is whether glamour should be reserved for the red carpet. For a few weeks, at least, the answer is no.
