Some resorts announce themselves. Amanvari, characteristically for Aman, does the opposite. Six years after it was first promised, the brand's debut Mexico property has finally opened on Baja California Sur's East Cape, a stretch of coastline so deliberately overlooked by the region's resort corridor that arriving here feels less like checking in and more like being let in on a secret.

The name itself gives the game away: Amanvari draws on the Sanskrit words for "peace" and "water", and the resort has been built at the precise point where those two ideas collide, where desert scrub meets a tidal estuary meets the improbably calm waters of the Sea of Cortez. Unlike the Pacific side of the peninsula, prone to currents and undertow, this coastline can actually be swum in, a detail so unglamorous it rarely makes the brochure, and so genuinely rare along this stretch of Mexico that it deserves top billing anyway.



![](/assets/images/posts/3-15-2018-7-15-44-pm.webp)



**Eighteen Casitas, and Nothing Else**

Aman resorts have always dealt in scarcity, but Amanvari takes the principle further than most. There are just eighteen standalone casitas here, each roughly 82 square metres, positioned along the shoreline, the estuary or tucked into a grove of palms, depending on the mood a guest is chasing. Some sit elevated above the landscape with sweeping views toward the Sierra de la Laguna mountains; others sit directly on the sand, close enough to hear the tide. Every casita comes with its own heated pool, an outdoor shower, and a terrace generous enough to make room service feel like the better option most evenings.

The architecture, led by Elastic Architects building on an original concept by Heah & Co, leans into restraint rather than spectacle: hand-applied white concrete, natural stone, timber and plaster, with a palette of terracotta, sand and warm neutral tones that reads as an extension of the desert rather than an imposition on it. Bespoke ceramics and original work by local artists appear throughout, a quiet reminder that this is a resort with a specific sense of place, not an imported one.



![](/assets/images/posts/4.-aman-residence.webp)



**Where Sea and Desert Meet**

Amanvari sits inside Costa Palmas, a 1,500-acre private estate that also includes eighteen acres of organic farms and orchards, a Robert Trent Jones II golf course, and a deep-water marina and yacht club, along with roughly three miles of genuinely swimmable beachfront. For a resort so focused on solitude, that shared infrastructure is a useful piece of practical luxury: horseback rides across coastal and desert terrain, private boat outings, snorkelling and diving trips to the coral reefs of Cabo Pulmo National Marine Park, and, depending on the season, whale watching in waters just offshore.

San José del Cabo International Airport is roughly an hour's drive away, or a brisk fifteen minutes by helicopter, with a dedicated private terminal easing the arrival for anyone unwilling to trade convenience for seclusion entirely.

**A Spa Built Around the Temazcal**

Wellness, as ever with Aman, is not an amenity here so much as an organising principle. The 660-square-metre Aman Spa is anchored by a contemporary reimagining of the temazcal, the traditional Mesoamerican sweat lodge, a first of its kind for the brand, alongside two Hydro Houses combining a banya and hammam, six private treatment rooms, a 24-hour fitness centre, a beauty salon with head spa services, and an open-air yoga pavilion. The stated intent, drawn from Mexico's ancestral healing traditions, is a kind of sequential transformation: the body purified, the mind released. Whether or not one buys entirely into the language, the setting does most of the convincing on its own.

**Four Tables, Four Moods**

Dining across the resort resists the trap of trying to be everything at once. Luma, the signature restaurant, is built around Baja ingredients and open-fire cooking, drawing on local seafood and produce grown on Costa Palmas's own farms. Sesui offers an intimate Japanese experience anchored by a ten-seat omakase counter, alongside a smaller à la carte dining room. Arva brings Aman's recurring Italian concept to the East Cape, while The Lounge and The Pool handle the more relaxed registers, cocktails at sunset, lunch with sand still on your feet.



![](/assets/images/posts/amanvari-mexico-supplied-1.jpg)



**The Cost of Quiet**

None of this comes cheaply. Opening rates run from roughly $3,000 to over $7,000 a night depending on season and casita, a figure that places Amanvari among the more expensive addresses in Mexico, let alone Baja. What that rate buys, beyond the obvious, is the thing Aman has always sold better than almost anyone else: the sense of a place built entirely around not being found. In a region increasingly defined by scale, that may end up being Amanvari's most luxurious offering of all.
