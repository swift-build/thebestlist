Some hotels are a place to sleep. Others are the reason you booked the trip.

India’s heritage stays belong firmly to the second group. They are buildings that were meant to impress, with domes, courtyards, jharokhas and ballrooms made for an audience of one court or one family. Many were saved from decline by restoration and given a second life as hotels, with the architecture left to do most of the talking.

This is a shortlist of the ones worth checking into, chosen because the building and the hospitality are equally compelling.

![](/assets/images/posts/samode-palace-3.webp)

**Samode Palace, near Jaipur**
In the village of Samode, about 40 km from Jaipur, this palace was the seat of the Rawals of Amber. Its mid-nineteenth-century additions include the frescoed Durbar Hall and the dazzling Sheesh Mahal, the Hall of Mirrors. It became a heritage hotel in 1987, and the Samode family still owns it. The hotel has 43 rooms and suites, and the family also runs Samode Haveli in Jaipur’s old city, another frescoed mansion with its own mirrored suite. Go for the mirrors and murals.

![](/assets/images/posts/0_tuiu1d.webp)

![](/assets/images/posts/311137052.jpg)



**Fateh Garh, Udaipur**
A hilltop heritage estate above the city, Fateh Garh has terraces that look across the valley to Lake Pichola and the City Palace. It has 56 rooms, two pools and five terraces. Its design follows Vastu principles, and guests can take a vintage car ride through Udaipur. Go for the view and the terraces at sunset.

![](/assets/images/posts/screenshot-2026-10-29-at-6.55.21 pm.png)

**Neemrana Fort-Palace, Rajasthan**
A fifteenth-century hilltop fort-palace in Alwar district, Neemrana rises in terraces down the hillside, with courtyards, balconies and stepped levels. It was restored and opened as a heritage hotel in the 1980s, helping start the conversation about reviving India’s crumbling monuments. It is a short drive from Delhi. Go for a weekend of stone, terraces and open sky.

**RAAS Devi Garh, near Udaipur**
An eighteenth-century palace set in the Aravalli hills in Delwara village, Devi Garh pairs its old bones with strikingly contemporary interiors. Go for the quiet, the landscape and a more minimalist take on palace living.

![](/assets/images/posts/vivaana.jpg)

![](/assets/images/posts/vivaana-1-.jpg)

**Vivaana Culture Hotel, Shekhawati**
Shekhawati is Rajasthan’s open-air gallery of painted havelis, and Vivaana lets you sleep inside one. The hotel occupies two restored nineteenth-century havelis in Churi Ajitgarh, near Mandawa, rich with frescoes. The name means the first rays of the rising sun. Alongside the courtyards sit a lawn, a pool and a spa. The group also runs Vivaana Museum Hotel in Nawalgarh, a small haveli property built around a working museum. Go for the frescoes and a slower pace.

**Stok Palace Heritage Hotel, Ladakh**
Built in 1820 as a summer home, Stok became the permanent residence of the Namgyal royal family after the Dogra conquest, and the family still lives there. About 15 km from Leh, the palace has six rooms with murals roughly two centuries old, and a museum of royal costumes, crowns and thangkas, some centuries old. It was opened to guests in 1980. Go for a living palace in the high Himalaya.



**Chidambara Vilas, near Pudukkottai**
A 118-year-old Chettiar home, Chidambara Vilas was restored over three years by the Sangam group. Period details do much of the work, from Athangudi tiles and hand-operated panka fans to vintage-style switches. Meals are served on banana leaves, and guests can join cooking classes. The Darbar Hall and Valavu courtyard are the centrepieces. Go for the details and the food.

**The Bangala, Karaikudi**
Housed in a roughly century-old bungalow that began as a family club, The Bangala became Chettinad’s first heritage hotel around 1999. It is family-run, closer to a grand home than a hotel, and famous for its kitchen, where meals feel like edible history. Guided tours take guests into the region’s mansions. Go for the cuisine and the sense of being a guest.
